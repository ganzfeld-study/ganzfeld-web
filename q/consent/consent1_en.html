Data and privacy policy

Volunteering

Participation is completely voluntary. It can be canceled at any time without need of an explanation and without consequences (e.g. grading if the participant is a student).

Data management and privacy

Data is protected according to the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). The processing of your personal data is necessary to carry out research that is of public interest (GDPR, Art. 6, p. 1e). We ask for names and email/phone numbers so that we can contact those invited to sessions 1 and 2 of the survey. The data we collect comes from your self-assessments on the forms where you will be asked to indicate the extent to which statements fit you, for example current attitude towards various alleged phenomena (such as parapsychological phenomena) and personality traits. We will collect data from many participants and report group results so that it is impossible to identify a person based on the information reported in research presentations. Those who participate in studies receive a number as a participant ID. This ID number will be linked to your name. Information linking your name to the study (e.g. the consent form) will be kept in a locked cabinet in our laboratory room, so that unauthorized people cannot access it, and will be saved for ten years after the end of the project. In order to make research as accessible and transparent as possible, the data will be posted online. We will not post data that can identify you such as name, education, or date.

The institution responsible for your personal data is Linnaeus University. According to the EU's data protection regulation, you have the right to receive free of charge access all the information about you obtained and, if necessary, to have any errors corrected. You can also request that data about you be deleted or that the processing of your personal data be restricted. Data can be destroyed upon request in connection with discontinuing participation in the study. However, the right to erase and limit the processing of personal data does not apply when the data is necessary for the current research. If you have questions about the studies, you can contact the investigators Etzel Cardeña and David Marcusson-Clavertz (see contact information below). The Linnaeus University's Data Protection Officer can be reached at 0470-70 85 03 dataskyddsombud@lnu.se. If you are dissatisfied with the way your personal data is processed, you have the right to lodge a complaint with the Swedish Data Protection Authority, which is the supervisory authority.
